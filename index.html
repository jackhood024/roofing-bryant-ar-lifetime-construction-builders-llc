Highlights

The parts of the roof

Decking. The OSB or plywood panels the roof sits on. A soft spot that flexes underfoot means replacement, not a patch.

Underlayment. The synthetic sheet under the shingles that keeps water off the wood.

Drip edge. The metal strip along eaves and rakes that sends water into the gutter, from Hurricane Creek to Bryant Parkway.

Step flashing. Small angled metal pieces where a roof meets a wall or chimney. A careful roofer checks them first when a leak shows near a wall.

Pipe boot. The rubber collar around a plumbing vent pipe. It cracks in the sun and is a usual leak source on a Saline County roof.

Valley. The V where two roof planes meet and the most water runs. Along the Alcoa Road corridor, debris in a valley is a leak waiting for the next hard rain.

Ridge cap and ridge vent. The cap covers the peak, where wind from summer thunderstorms tears first, and the vent beneath lets hot attic air escape.

Materials and methods

Tear-off. Stripping the old roof down to bare decking. Skipping it means new shingles over soft wood.

Granules. The mineral coating on asphalt shingles, found at the bottom of a downspout.

Standing seam. Metal panels joined at raised seams with concealed fasteners. Lifetime Construction Builders offers 24-gauge snap-lock and mechanical-lock standing seam in Galvalume.

R-panel. An exposed-fastener metal panel, 26-gauge. The screws loosen over the years and need re-screwing.

Stone coated steel. Steel panels in tile, shake and shingle profiles, made by DECRA, TILCOR and Westlake Royal. It takes longer to install than shingle.

TPO, EPDM, PVC. The three flat-roof membranes on commercial buildings. All three fail at open seams, punctures and ponding water.

Written estimate. A price on paper that states the scope of the work. The verbal kind is where surprise bills come from.
